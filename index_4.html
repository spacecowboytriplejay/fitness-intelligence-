How this works

Nthato, you do not need motivation. You need a system you cannot argue with and a person who will notice when you skip. This document is the system. I am the person.

The physics is simple. The belly goes when you eat slightly less than you burn for long enough. The arms come when you lift progressively heavier for long enough while eating enough protein. Everything else in here exists to make "long enough" survivable for a man with a job, a wife and small children.

The morning: God, mind, then body

Every man in this program has a body plan. Almost none of them have a plan for the first 30 minutes of the day, which is the only part of the day that is fully yours before the kids, the phone and the office take the rest. The morning is where the leader is built. The gym is only where it shows.

The sequence is fixed. The lengths are flexible. On a night when the kids were up four times, do the five-minute version and do not feel bad about it. The point is that it happens every day, in this order, before the phone is unlocked.

The three lines (the mindset system)

Mindset programs fail because they are vague. This one is three sentences, handwritten, every morning, under today's date. They take three minutes and they compound for 180 days.

Line 1, identity: "I am a man who ______." Finish it with the man you are becoming, in the present tense, in your own words. Examples: "I am a man who keeps his word to himself." "I am a man whose children see him train." Rotate as needed, but keep it about who you are, never about what you weigh.

Line 2, the principle for today: pick one of your principles (below) and write how it will show up today, specifically. Not "be disciplined". Rather: "Discipline: I train at 17:30 even though I will not feel like it, and I eat the cottage cheese at 21:00 instead of the biscuits."

Line 3, the one thing: the single action that, if it is the only thing you do today, makes today a win. Work, family or body, your choice. One, not three.

In the evening, on the second walk, you answer one question in your head: did I live the three lines? If yes, good. If no, no guilt, tomorrow's lines get written with more honesty. The Sunday check-in asks how many days out of seven you wrote them.

  • Stewardship. This body is on loan. I maintain it the way I would maintain anything entrusted to me.

  • Strength is for service. I get strong so that I can carry more for the people who depend on me, not so that I can be looked at.

The two walks

Two walks a day are mandatory and non-negotiable, the same as the Sunday check-in. Morning walk after prayer and the Word, evening walk after dinner. Ten minutes minimum, twenty is better. No phone in hand; if it must come, it stays in the pocket on silent. The evening walk is the family version if you want it: wife, kids, the dog. It is also the single best tool against the 21:30 fridge visit, because it moves the body through the exact window where the snacking happens and it settles the stomach after dinner.

Together the two walks deliver most of your daily step target, clear the head twice a day, give you two guaranteed slots where you are not being pulled by a screen, and set the sleep clock at both ends of the day. On gym days the walks still happen; the gym does not replace them.

Week zero: the baseline

You cannot manage what you have not measured. Before week one, collect the following and send it to me. This becomes the "before" that makes day 180 satisfying.

  • Morning weight, seven days in a row. Same scale, after the bathroom, before food. Send the seven-day average, not one number. A single weigh-in is noise.
  • Waist at the navel, relaxed, tape parallel to the floor. This is the belly metric. It moves faster than the scale and matters more.
  • Three photos: front, side, back. Same light, same spot, morning. You never have to send these to anyone, but take them. Day 90 you will thank yourself.
  • Strength benchmarks, done in week one: goblet squat, incline dumbbell press, lat pulldown, dumbbell Romanian deadlift, incline dumbbell curl. Note the weight you can do for 10 clean reps.
  • Sleep: average hours per night over the week. Be honest. Small kids make this the hardest variable in the whole program.
  • Your principles and a notebook. Buy one paper notebook. Write your five to seven principles on the first page and send me a photo of it. The three lines go in it every morning from day one.
  • Blood work (optional, high value): ask your wife about a basic panel: lipids, fasting glucose or HbA1c, vitamin D, total testosterone, thyroid. Repeat at day 180. This turns "I feel better" into proof.

The rules that do the heavy lifting

  • No liquid calories except a protein shake. Juice, sugary drinks, milky coffees and beer are the belly's best friends. Coffee is black or with a splash. Zero-sugar drinks are allowed.
  • Alcohol cap: four drinks per week, never more than two in a day. Alcohol pauses fat burning while it is metabolised and wrecks sleep quality. If you want faster results, go to zero for the first 30 days and watch the waist tape.
  • Vegetables at two meals, minimum. Volume is what keeps you full in a deficit. Aim for the fibre number in the calculator.
  • Weigh, do not guess, for the first 30 days. A kitchen scale costs less than one takeaway. After 30 days your eye is calibrated and you can relax to eyeballing on weekends.
  • One meal a week is off-plan. Not a day. A meal. Sit down, enjoy it, no guilt, back on at the next meal. Planned, it costs you nothing. Unplanned, it becomes a weekend.
  • Eat slowly, protein and vegetables first, carbs last. Fullness signals take 15 to 20 minutes to arrive. Order of eating changes how much you eat.

The hacks

  • The afternoon energy drink. A 150 mg caffeine, zero-sugar energy drink (an NPL or similar) at 14:00 to 15:00 kills the afternoon snack window and powers the evening session. Hard cutoff: nothing with caffeine after 15:00, or your sleep pays for it.
  • The Saturday protein bar. One good protein bar as the weekend treat. It scratches the sweet itch, hits 20 g protein and costs 200 to 250 kcal instead of the 800 a slab of chocolate costs.
  • Sparkling water and a big glass before every meal. Cheapest appetite tool that exists.
  • Sunday batch cook. 1 kg chicken, 1 kg rice, a tray of roasted vegetables. Ninety minutes on Sunday buys you five lunches you never have to think about.
  • Brush your teeth after dinner. Sounds silly. Ends the eating day in your head.
  • Hunger is not an emergency. In a deficit you will feel hungry for 20 minutes, two or three times a day. Drink water, walk, it passes. Real hunger that does not pass means you are under-eating protein or fibre, so message me.

Three gym sessions a week, 50 to 60 minutes each. Two bike sessions. One optional 20-minute arm and core session at home with the dumbbells. Steps every day. That is the whole structure. Daily steps: the hidden lever

Eight thousand steps a day minimum, ten thousand on rest days. The two mandatory walks get you most of the way there. Walking burns 300 to 400 kcal a day at this level without touching recovery, and it is the difference between a program that works and one that stalls in week six. Pram walks, walking meetings, parking far away: it all counts. The phone tracks it.

Bike sessions

Zone 2 means you can hold a conversation but would rather not. On a static bike that is usually 60 to 70 percent of max heart rate, roughly 110 to 130 beats per minute for a 40-year-old. Thirty minutes, steady, boring. Boring is the point: it burns fat, builds the heart and does not steal recovery from the lifting. From week nine, once a week, replace the last 10 minutes with 5 rounds of 30 seconds hard, 90 seconds easy.

How every gym session runs

  1. Warm-up, 6 minutes. 3 minutes on the bike, then 10 bodyweight squats, 10 push-ups, 10 band or cable face pulls, 10 arm circles each way.
  2. Ramp-up sets. For the first exercise of the session, do one set at half the working weight for 8 reps, then one at 75 percent for 5 reps, then start the working sets.
  3. Working sets at the listed RPE. RPE 7 means you could do 3 more reps. RPE 8 means 2 more. RPE 9 means 1 more. Beginners always underestimate how hard they can push and overestimate how hard they are pushing. Film a set now and then.
  4. Rest exactly as listed. Use the phone timer. Rest is where the strength comes from.
  5. Log every set in a notes app or a free app like Hevy or Strong: exercise, weight, reps. The logbook is the program.

The progression rule (this is how the biceps grow)

Double progression. Every exercise has a rep range, for example 8 to 12. Start at a weight where you can do 8 reps at the listed RPE. Each session, try to add a rep to each set. When you hit the top of the range (12) on every set, add weight next session: 2.5 kg on upper body dumbbells (that is 2.5 kg per hand), 5 kg on lower body and machines. Then you are back to 8 reps and the climb starts again. That is it. Do this for 180 days and you will be a different man.

If you fail to add a rep for three sessions in a row on the same exercise: check sleep, check protein, and tell me. We will swap the exercise or the rep range.

The travel and chaos protocol

Some weeks fall apart. Sick child, work trip, a wife on night shift. The protocol for a broken week is: hit protein every day, walk 8,000 steps, do two 20-minute home sessions (push-ups, goblet squats, dumbbell rows, curls, plank), and do not try to make up missed sessions. A maintenance week costs you nothing. A guilt spiral costs you a month.

Recovery: the part nobody sells you

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. For a father of small children the honest constraint is sleep, so the recovery plan is designed around broken nights rather than pretending they will not happen.

Sleep

  • Target seven hours, protect the first five. Lights out by 22:00 on gym nights. If the kids wake you at 04:30, you still banked the deep sleep that matters for recovery.
  • Caffeine cutoff 15:00. Half of a 150 mg drink is still in your blood at 21:00.
  • No screens in bed. Not for the blue light, for the 45 minutes it steals.
  • Bad night rule. Under five hours of sleep: still train, but cap every set at RPE 7 and drop the last set of each exercise. Two bad nights in a row: swap the gym session for a walk. Recovery debt compounds faster than training gains.

Joints and tendons after 35

  • Elbows. Curl volume climbs through the program. If the inside of the elbow starts to ache, drop the barbell curl for hammer curls for two weeks and tell me. Do not push through tendon pain, ever.
  • Lower back. Brace before every squat, deadlift and row: big breath into the belly, tighten as if someone is about to punch you, then move. The dead bugs and planks are there for this reason.
  • Shoulders. Face pulls every week for the rest of your life. They fix the desk posture that ruins pressing.
  • Warm up properly. The six-minute warm-up is not optional after 35. Cold tendons tear; warm ones adapt.

Stress

Cortisol from work and broken sleep makes the belly stubborn and the appetite loud. The bike sessions and daily walks are as much stress management as fat burning. Ten minutes outside without a phone after lunch is worth more than one more set of anything.

Questions you will ask, answered now

Will I lose muscle in the deficit?

Not with 2 g per kg of protein and progressive lifting. As a beginner you will build muscle and lose fat at the same time for most of the first 90 days. That window closes as you get more trained, which is why we use it now.

Can I train arms more? I really want the biceps.

I know. The program already hits biceps directly two to three times a week plus every pull. Extra curls now buy you sore elbows, not bigger arms. Chin-ups and rows with the progression rule will do more for your arms than any curl variation. Once you can do 8 clean chin-ups, we talk about a dedicated arm day.

What supplements?

Whey protein (convenience), creatine monohydrate 5 g daily (the most evidence-backed supplement there is, helps strength and muscle, ask your wife, it is safe for healthy kidneys), vitamin D if your bloods are low, caffeine as planned. Nothing else is worth your money.

The gym is closed or I only have the dumbbells at home.

Run the home session plus goblet squats, dumbbell Romanian deadlifts, one-arm rows and push-ups. Twenty-five minutes. The progression rule still applies.

I fell off for a week.

Message me. Do not restart from week one. Pick up where the calendar says you should be, at slightly lighter weights, and you will be back to your numbers within two sessions. Never miss twice.
